# Cops & Robbers: Core Gameplay Brainstorm

Round 1 of ideas. Each section offers options with trade-offs; my pick is marked ★.

---

## 1. The big idea: "preparation leaks the plan"

Robbers can't just walk to the bank. Every **goal card** names a target and the **prep items** needed to pull it off, e.g.

| Target | Needs |
|---|---|
| Bank | Drill (Hardware Store) · Uniform (Laundromat) · Blueprints (Town Hall) |
| Museum | Glass Cutter (Hardware Store) · Disguise (Theatre) · Guard Schedule (Café) |
| Jewellery Store | Glass Cutter · Getaway Car (Garage) · Blueprints |

Robbers must visit the shops that supply those items, and **cops watch where they go**. Targets share items on purpose, so one visit never gives the game away: going to the Hardware Store points to the Bank, the Museum *or* the Jewellery Store. This is the deduction engine (a bit like Clue, but the suspects are buildings).

Ways to keep it uncertain:
- **Face-down pickups.** Each supply tile offers 2 or 3 item types; cops see the robber was there but not what they took.
- **Decoys.** Robbers can grab useless items to throw cops off, at the cost of time.
- **Shared overlap.** Design the goal deck so every item fits at least 2 or 3 targets.

## 2. Turn structure

**★ Option A: "Days before the heist" (simple and family-friendly)**
Each round is one day:
1. **Robbers act.** Each robber moves and takes one action at their tile (pick up an item, scout, lie low).
2. **Cops act.** Each cop moves and takes one action (patrol, stake out, question a witness, set a roadblock).
3. **Event card** (optional): a parade blocks a street, a bank holiday, a museum gala...
4. **Clock advances.**

**Option B: Alternating pawns.** Robber, cop, robber, cop... More interactive and chase-like, but slower at 6 players.

**Option C: Simultaneous planning.** Robbers secretly program their moves with cards, then reveal. Tense, but heavier than a family game probably wants.

## 3. How the robbers communicate

This is the trickiest design question. Options:

- **★ Everyone knows, talk is public.** All robbers see the goal card. They can say anything out loud, but cops hear it all, so the talk turns into bluffing ("Let's hit the museum tonight!"). Very family-friendly and makes for funny moments.
- **One mastermind.** Only one robber knows the target and steers the others with limited hints (a hint card, or a single word per round). Good puzzle, but the other robbers can feel like passengers.
- **Split knowledge.** Each robber holds part of the plan (one knows the district, another the type of building, another the item list). They have to coordinate through *actions* rather than words. Clever, but harder for younger players.
- **Secret signals.** Robbers get a small hand of "plan cards" (Hurry, Decoy, Split up, Regroup) they can pass face-down to each other.

## 4. Cop tools

Cops need ways to turn suspicion into evidence:
- **Stakeout.** Put a token on a tile. If a robber takes an item there, they must reveal it.
- **Witness / informant card.** Draw a clue that rules out one target ("It's not anything on the east side of town").
- **Roadblock.** Block a street for a round, which slows prep or forces a detour.
- **Arrest.** If a cop catches a robber, the robber **drops one item face-up** and goes back to the hideout. Catching someone is the best source of information, which makes chasing worthwhile.
- **CCTV tiles.** Some buildings reveal anything picked up there automatically.

## 5. The unanimous accusation

- Each cop secretly picks a target card, then all cops reveal together. **They must all match.**
- Cops can talk it over first (and robbers can chip in with misleading opinions!).
- A mismatch or a wrong guess costs something: lose an accusation token, advance the robbers' clock, or free up a robber.
- **Limited accusations** (e.g. 2 per game) keep the cops from guessing every round.
- In 1v1 unanimity is automatic, so give the lone cop a single accusation, or make them commit one round ahead.

## 6. Win conditions and the clock

**★ Option A: Race.** Robbers win if they collect all their items, reach the target and announce the heist. Cops win by accusing correctly first, or if dawn comes (round limit, e.g. 8 to 10 rounds) before the robbers pull it off.

**Option B: Lockdown, then scramble.** A correct accusation locks down the target, and robbers must draw a backup goal card and keep going with less time left. Longer game, but no sudden ending.

**Option C: Score loot vs arrests.** Several smaller heists per game, with points. Less tense, but nobody is knocked out early.

**Built-in tension:** robbers want to move quickly (dawn is coming), but every visit gives cops information. Moving fast means giving more away; moving slowly means running out of time.

## 7. The modular town

- Tile types: **targets** (Bank, Museum, Town Hall, Jewellery Store, Casino...), **suppliers** (Hardware Store, Theatre, Garage, Café...) and **specials** (Police Station, Robbers' Hideout, Park, Train Station for fast travel).
- **Filter the goal deck** so only targets actually on the board can come up.
- **Setup draft:** teams take turns placing tiles. Robbers want suppliers spread out and confusing; cops want them near the station. That gives you a short strategic phase before play starts.
- Distance matters: a target far from the station is easier to rob but slower to prep.

## 8. Scaling to 2–6 players

**★ Fixed crew, shared out.** Always 3 robber pawns and 3 cop pawns (or 2 and 2 for a shorter game). Teams divide pawns among themselves:

| Players | Each player controls |
|---|---|
| 2 (1v1) | 3 pawns |
| 4 (2v2) | 1–2 pawns |
| 6 (3v3) | 1 pawn |

Balance stays the same at every player count, so you only have to tune it once. The alternative (one pawn per player, so pawn counts scale) is simpler to explain but needs separate balancing for each count.

## 9. Family-friendly touches

- No violence: "caught" means sent back to the hideout.
- Fun characters with one special ability each (the Locksmith skips one item, the Detective draws 2 clues, the Getaway Driver moves double).
- Event cards for silly moments (the mayor's cat escapes, the ice cream truck jams Main Street).
