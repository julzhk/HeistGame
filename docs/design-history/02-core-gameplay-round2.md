# Cops & Robbers: Brainstorm Round 2

**Decided so far:** simultaneous secret planning, about 45 minutes, a smart 8-year-old can play, and double agents are in.

---

## 1. Simultaneous planning that an 8-year-old can handle

> **✅ Update (julz): pawns stay on the board and move openly.** The Where *cards* are replaced by **destination tokens placed on the board** (see below). What cards stay in hand as before.
>
> **Destination tokens:** each pawn has a matching coloured token with a plain back. In the Plan step, each team places its tokens **face-down on the tiles** their pawns are heading to (up to 2 tiles away). Each team also has **2 blank decoy tokens** it can place as well. On Reveal, flip all the tokens and walk each pawn to its token.
> - Cops can see *roughly* where the robbers are heading (5 face-down tokens, 3 real), so there's something to read, guess and bluff around before the reveal.
> - It's all on the board and easy to see, which suits an 8-year-old.
> - Decoys are reusable. Robbers might get one extra as a "crafty" bonus. Leave decoys out in Rookie Rules.
>
> Wherever "Where card" appears below, read it as "destination token".

The aim is to keep the guessing tension without making the rules hard to follow.

**★ Recommended: "Where and What" planning**

Every tile in the town has a big letter or picture. Each pawn has **two small face-down cards** to plan with each round:
1. **Where:** a tile card for where the pawn is going, up to 2 tiles away.
2. **What:** an action card for what it does when it gets there.

| Robber actions | Cop actions |
|---|---|
| Grab (take an item) | Search (spot what robbers grabbed here) |
| Scout (peek at a clue or event) | Roadblock (nobody can pass this tile next round) |
| Lie low (can't be caught this round) | Stakeout (leave a watcher token behind) |
| Heist! (only at the target, once all items are collected) | Chase (catch any robber on this tile or next to it) |

**Each round (about 4 minutes):**
1. **Plan.** Everyone puts down their Where and What cards at the same time. Teams can whisper among themselves.
2. **Reveal.** Flip all the cards together and move the pawns.
3. **Resolve, in a fixed order:** Chase → Roadblock and Stakeout → Grab and Scout → Heist.
4. **Clock.** Move the dawn marker one space.

Why it works:
- Everything is simultaneous, so cops have to **guess where robbers will go**, and robbers have to guess where cops will lie in wait. That's where the tension comes from.
- Each pawn has only two choices each round: a destination token and an action card. *(✅ julz: target age is now a **smart 12-year-old**, since the game as a whole is more complex. That leaves room for character abilities, Shady Deals and spent-card tracking in the standard game, and Rookie Rules covers younger players.)*
- Fixed resolution order means no rules arguments.
- 8 rounds × 4 minutes ≈ 32 minutes, plus setup and the finale, comes to about 45 minutes.

### Standard rule: action cards are spent ✅ (julz's call)
Each **team** has its own hand of action cards. Once a card has been played, it goes **face-up** into that team's used pile. Hands refill only at **shift change**, after rounds 3 and 6 (mark them on the dawn track).

| Robber hand | Cop hand |
|---|---|
| Grab ×3, Scout ×2, Lie low ×1 | Search ×2, Chase ×2, Roadblock ×1, Stakeout ×1 |

- **Heist!** is never used up. It's a single card the robbers keep and play only once.
- **Always free:** any pawn can play just a Where card and move without taking an action, so a team never gets stuck with nothing to do.
- **Why it's good:** the used piles are public information. If the cops have spent both Chase cards, robbers can move more boldly until shift change. If the robbers have used all their Grabs, cops know a heist can't happen yet. Planning *when* to spend a card becomes part of the strategy, and it's still easy to understand.
- These numbers are a first guess and will need playtesting. If robbers get too few Grabs before shift change, the heist can't happen in time.

### Easier variant: "Rookie Rules"
For younger or first-time players: every pawn has the full set of action cards every round, and nothing is used up. Planning is the same; there's just less to keep track of.

## 2. Double agents

There's one design trap here. **Cops must accuse unanimously, so a mole on the cop team can block every accusation.** Each version below needs a counter to that.

### Option A: ★ "Maybe there's a mole" (loyalty cards)
- At setup, each team secretly draws loyalty cards from a small deck. **Each team might or might not have a turncoat.** For example, the cops shuffle 3 Loyal and 1 Turncoat card and deal 1 to each cop, so there's a 1-in-4 chance of a turncoat on each side.
- **Turncoat cop:** secretly a robber. They peek at the goal card at the start and win with the robbers. Their aim is to steer the investigation the wrong way.
- **Undercover robber:** secretly a cop. They know the target like the other robbers, and their aim is to leak it through their plays: dawdling, grabbing the wrong items, or "accidentally" walking into a Chase.
- The chance that **nobody is a traitor** keeps it from feeling like a witch hunt. Everyone stays a little paranoid.

### Option B: "Internal Affairs" (the counter to blocking)
- Once per game, a team can call Internal Affairs and vote, by majority, to **expose one teammate**. That player shows their loyalty card.
  - Traitor caught: they're benched (their pawns become neutral) and their real team loses a bonus.
  - Loyal player wrongly accused: the other team gets a bonus. For cops that means the clock jumps; for robbers it means a free item.
- A mole who keeps voting against the others starts to look suspicious, which gives cops a reason to use Internal Affairs.

### Option C: "Bribe" (turning someone during play)
Robbers collect **bribe tokens** at some tiles (the Casino, for instance). They can spend 3 to slip a "You've been bought" card to one cop, who then secretly plays for the robbers. More dramatic, but it means a player switches sides partway through, which may upset younger players.

### Option D: "Snitch" (one-time betrayal)
Each robber has a single Snitch card. Playing it makes them leave the gang: they reveal the target to the cops and win alone if the cops then succeed. A short burst of drama, but it rewards selfish play in a family game.

### ✅ julz's call: all four are in, arranged in layers
Using all four at full strength every game could swamp a 45-minute game and confuse an 8-year-old. So they come in layers:

1. **Always on (4+ players):** Loyalty cards (A) and Internal Affairs (B). Together they form the paranoid core, and Internal Affairs keeps a mole from blocking every accusation.
2. **"Shady Deals" cards, mixed into the event deck:** each game deals out only a few events, so Bribe (C) and Snitch (D) may or may not turn up. Every game feels a bit different.
   - **Bribe:** when this card comes up, robbers at the Casino can pay a token to slip a "You've been bought" card to one cop.
   - **Snitch:** when this card comes up, one robber is given a Snitch card in secret and can choose to play it later.
3. **Traitor cap:** at most **1 traitor in 2v2** and **2 in 3v3**, counting moles, bribed cops and snitches together. Once the cap is reached, any Bribe or Snitch card that comes up is ignored. This stops a team from being entirely traitors.
4. **1v1:** no double agents.

Showing who is secretly on which side: each player keeps their loyalty card under their player board. A bribed cop swaps theirs for the "bought" card. At the end of the game everyone flips their cards over for the big reveal.

## 3. Win conditions (✅ race + Lone Wolf; no points, no lockdown)

**Game length (working assumption):** around **60 minutes**, because 45 felt too short now that double agents are in. That means **10 rounds** with shift changes after rounds 3, 6 and 8. It's the dawn track that stretches, not the number of win conditions.

Sudden-death and race formats fit a 45-minute game best.

**★ Recommended:**
- **Robbers win** if they play Heist! at the right target with all the items collected.
- **Cops win** if they accuse unanimously and correctly, **or** if dawn arrives (end of round 8) before a heist.
- **Wrong accusation:** the dawn marker goes back one space, giving robbers more time. Cops get only 2 accusations per game.
- **Double agents** win with their true team, so a turncoat cop celebrates when the heist succeeds.

**✅ "Lone Wolf" last gasp (julz's call: only one robber can win this way)**
- When the cops make the final correct accusation (or at dawn), any **single robber** can make a solo run, but only if the items **that pawn is carrying** match a backup target.
- If several robbers qualify, they announce at the same time. If more than one wants to run, the one closest to their backup target goes.
- The runner gets **one round**: move up to 2 tiles and play Heist!. The cops get a free round of Chase cards against that pawn, even if they've used theirs up.
- **If the run works, only that player wins.** The cops lose, and so do the runner's teammates.
- **What this changes during the game:** robbers have a reason to hold on to items personally instead of sharing them, which makes them slightly less trusting of each other. Combined with the Snitch, the gang is less united than it looks.
- **1v1:** one player controls all the robber pawns, so a Lone Wolf win is just a robber win. It's still a comeback chance.

**What I'd avoid:** a full points system, or a lockdown followed by a whole new target. Both push the game past 45 minutes and blunt the finale.
