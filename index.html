Cops & Robbers

One town. One secret target.
Ten nights until dawn.

The robbers know exactly what they're after. The cops don't, and every one of them has to agree before they can slap on the cuffs. Every shop the gang visits is a clue, every move is planned in secret, and every card you spend leaves a trail.

Pick a side

Two teams. One of them is lying.

Split the table into two even teams. Each team always moves a crew of exactly 3 pawns, so the game plays the same whether you're 2, 4 or 6. Every pawn moves openly on the board. What it's actually up to is the secret.

The crew · Rosa · Benny · Ziggy

The Robbers

You've drawn a secret job: the Bank, the Museum, the Jewellery Store, the Casino or the Grand Hotel. Now get the three tools it needs without making it obvious.

  • Grab gear around town: a Drill from the Hardware Store, a Disguise from the Theatre, a Guard Schedule from the all-night diner.
  • Bluff. Grab things you don't need. Drop decoy tokens on the wrong targets. Say anything you like.
  • Strike. Get a robber onto the target with all 3 items in the crew and play Heist! You win, instantly.
The law · Dot · Ray · Kim

The Cops

You don't know the target. But you know what every target needs, and you can see where the crew has been shopping.

  • Search and stake out suppliers to see exactly what got grabbed.
  • Chase suspects and throw down roadblocks. Caught robbers drop evidence.
  • Accuse. Every cop secretly names a target. If you all agree and you're right, you win. You only get 2 tries.

And if the sun comes up after Hour 10 with no heist? The cops win. It's a race, and the clock is on the law's side.

How to play

One night in Harbour Hill

Both teams plan at the same time, whispering in huddles. Then everything flips at once. It's a game of reading the board, guessing what the other side is guessing, and holding your nerve.

  1. Briefing

    The cops may make an accusation. Everyone may talk. Robbers may lie through their teeth.

  2. Plan

    Each team puts a face-down destination token on the board for every pawn (up to 2 tiles away), plus decoys, and a face-down action card.

  3. Reveal

    Flip everything together. Gasp. Walk each pawn to its token.

  4. Resolve

    Always in order: Chase → Roadblock & Stakeout → Grab, Search & Scout → Heist! No arguments.

  5. Dawn

    Flip an event (Street Parade! Power Cut!) and move the dawn marker one night closer to sunrise.

The dawn track. Shift changes after Nights 3, 6 and 8 refill both teams' hands. After Night 10, the sun comes up.

A new town every game

Build the scene of the crime

Harbour Hill is made of 20 noir city blocks, and each game uses 16 of them in a 4×4 grid. Every edge matches, so any layout works. Change the town and you change which jobs are even possible. Go on, deal one.

Harbour Hill, as seen on Night 8.

Try it yourself

Can you crack the case?

You're a cop. Read the evidence, then accuse. You can name a suspect any time, but a wrong accusation gives the robbers more time. Fewer clues, more glory.

Learn it like a criminal career

Five ways to play

The rules come in five modes. Each one adds a layer to the one before and plays as a complete game. Start with Break-In, then climb the ladder.

MODE 1

Petty Theft

The core race. Learn it in 20 minutes.

Start hereMODE 2

Break-In

Chases, stakeouts, roadblocks, decoys and events. Every action, every night.

Standard gameMODE 3

The Big Job

A shared team hand that runs out. Your used pile is a clue for the other side.

MODE 4

Inside Job

Secret loyalty cards, Internal Affairs and Shady Deals. Is the cop beside you on the take?

MODE 5

Lone Wolf

The cops guessed right? One robber holding a full set for a different job makes a solo getaway, and wins alone.

Designer's notes

Why it works (we hope)

Open moves, hidden intent

Pawns walk around the board in plain sight. The secret is why. Face-down destination tokens and decoys give the cops something to read, and the robbers something to bluff with.

The prep leaks the plan

Every target needs three items and every item fits more than one target. Robbers can't avoid leaving clues, only blur them. That's the whole deduction engine, and it fits on one card.

Unanimous, with a mole

Cops must agree to accuse, which is tense on its own. Add a possible double agent who can quietly block the vote, and Internal Affairs to flush them out, and the precinct gets very twitchy.
